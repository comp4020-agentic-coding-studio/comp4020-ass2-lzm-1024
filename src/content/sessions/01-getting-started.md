## Before the session

Bring a recording device and, if possible, headphones. Watch one short video you admire twice and note where the image or sound changes.

## In the session

We unpack the Week 1 lecture, practise wide, medium and close shots, then work in pairs to record six shots of a routine changing. Keep the footage; it becomes material for next week’s review.

## Afterwards

Choose the strongest three shots and write one sentence answering: what is different by the final frame?

## Reference viewing

Watch a 60–90 second non-fiction video and list each shot change. Which change first tells you where you are?

## Further reading and viewing

These optional resources extend this week’s ideas. Take one useful observation from each resource into your viewing notebook; do not try to memorise rules.

- [Composition 101: Part 1 (Videomaker)](https://www.videomaker.com/article/c10/12190-composition-101-part-1) introduces composition as a storytelling decision. Use it to identify one framing choice in a short video you watch this week.
- [Exploring the language of film (National Film and Sound Archive of Australia)](https://shop.nfsa.gov.au/assets/files/Story%20of%20Rosy%20Dock_TN.pdf) provides a simple worksheet linking image, sound and camera movement. Use the film-language table on page 8 to annotate your Week 1 field exercise.
- [Making Audio and Video Media Accessible (W3C)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/) explains why captions, transcripts and visual description need to be considered during planning rather than added at the end. Read the overview before recording your first assessed work.
