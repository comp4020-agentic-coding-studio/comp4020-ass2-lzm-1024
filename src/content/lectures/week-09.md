A captioned edit is not a compliance afterthought — it is the version most of your audience will actually receive, muted, on a phone, in public. This lecture works through caption craft (reading-speed timing, minimum duration, phrase-based line breaks, safe areas), the different jobs music and ambient sound do in non-fiction work, and why aspect ratio and duration are decisions made at the shoot, not in the export.

## Outline

We start from the distinction between subtitles (speech only) and captions (speech plus meaningful non-speech audio), then work through the timing rules that make a caption legible rather than merely present, and open versus closed captioning trade-offs. From there we compare music's connotative, emotion-cueing role against ambient sound's evidentiary "you are here" function, including the risk of music overriding what the image documents, and a short pass on music rights for student work. The session closes on aspect ratio and duration as audience-context decisions, and ties both back to this week's practical milestone: building a final-project shoot schedule and re-confirming every permission it depends on.

Bring a 30-second test sequence from the final project and its current caption draft.
