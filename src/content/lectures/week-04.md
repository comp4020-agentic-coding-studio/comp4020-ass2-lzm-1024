Sound is captured, not caught by accident — the recording chain from source
through microphone and gain to recorder, the choice between cardioid,
shotgun and lavalier patterns, and closed-back monitoring all decide whether
a viewer can tell where they are and what matters. This lecture works
through gain staging and why clipping is unrecoverable, room tone as a
mandatory ambient bed, wind noise and windscreen choice, and sound
perspective as sound's version of Week 3's light continuity across a cut.

## Outline

We build the recording chain stage by stage — source, microphone pattern,
gain, recorder — then compare cardioid, shotgun and lavalier microphones
against proximity effect and direct-to-reverberant ratio. Monitoring on
headphones, room tone capture and wind protection round out the technical
core before we deconstruct the sound-portrait exercise: establish place with
an ambient bed, foreground a voice or sound, then resolve.

Bring headphones and a ten-second recording of ambient sound from your own
space.
