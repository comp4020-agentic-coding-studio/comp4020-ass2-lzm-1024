# Visual Storytelling for Short-Form Media

## Week 1: Seeing before shooting

---

# A short video needs a change

It may be a change in light, attention, understanding, movement or mood.

---

# The story question

What is different by the final frame?

---

# Shot size guides attention

- **Wide shot**: context and geography
- **Medium shot**: action and relationships
- **Close-up**: detail, emotion and evidence

---

# Composition is emphasis

Choose where the viewer looks first. Use framing, contrast, foreground and empty space to keep the subject legible.

---

# Sound carries place

Record clear voice when dialogue matters. Capture ambient sound before you leave. Use music only when you have permission.

---

# Editing creates pace

Select useful takes, arrange them for clarity, refine duration and sound, then export a version another person can watch.

---

# Ethics before publication

Ask permission. Preserve context. Do not turn someone’s distress into a more dramatic image.

---

# Week 1 field exercise

In pairs, record six shots of an ordinary routine changing. Include one wide shot, one medium shot, one close-up and ten seconds of ambient sound.

---

# Make one deliberate minute of footage

Start with an ordinary action. Decide what should change. Record with enough care that another person can follow it.

---

# Today’s lecture

1. Observe a change
2. Plan the viewer’s experience
3. Assemble a clear sequence

---

# Light establishes tone

Front light shows faces clearly. Side light adds shape. Back light separates a subject from its background.

---

# A simple editing workflow

Select usable takes. Arrange shots for clarity. Refine duration and sound. Export a version another person can watch.

---

# Assignment 1: A small change

Create a 60–90 second non-fiction visual story. Show a recognisable beginning and ending. Submit a captioned MP4 and a one-page production record.

---

# The 12-week course arc

The course moves from camera and phone control to light, sound, interview, planning, rough cuts, peer review and final screening.

---

# Shooting checklist

- Can you explain the change in one sentence?
- Do the first and last frames have a purpose?
- Did you capture clear sound and a backup copy of your files?

---

# Critique vocabulary

Use **framing**, **rhythm**, **continuity** and **point of view** to describe an observable choice before offering a suggestion.

---

# What to bring next week

Bring a phone or camera, headphones if you have them, and three selected shots from the field exercise.

---

# Key ideas from Week 1

A short video needs a point of view and a change. Image, sound and editing guide attention. Consent and context shape responsible publication.
